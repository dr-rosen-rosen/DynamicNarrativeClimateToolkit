## The question

**Based only on what this narrative conveys, how favourably does it portray the organisation's safety practice?**

Safety practice = the state of safety as practised in that workplace: hazard recognition and control, procedures and whether they are followed, supervision, training and competence, communication and coordination, equipment and housekeeping, and the organisation's response to events (investigation, correction, learning).

## The scale

| Rating | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 1 — Strongly unfavourable | More than a single frontline lapse: a known hazard left uncontrolled, a repeated problem, supervision that ignored it, or a dismissive or concealing response. |
| 2 — Somewhat unfavourable | A single lapse or deficiency is stated (inadequate precaution, missing control, non-compliance), with no response or only a required one. |
| 3 — Mixed | **Both** a stated lapse **and** a discretionary response or a control that worked, in the same narrative. |
| 4 — Somewhat favourable | Practice shown working: hazard recognised and controlled, procedure followed, a discretionary response, corrective steps that change how the work is done, a potential risk identified before it caused harm. |
| 5 — Strongly favourable | Proactive, conservative practice: stop-work or conservative decisions, in-depth analysis or investigation, learning actions, explicit management commitment. |
| CJ — Can't judge | No information about safety practice (mechanism of injury or failure only, or only required acts). |

**Use the full scale within each industry.** Reserve 1 for the clearly worst narratives you see in that industry and 5 for the clearly best. Analysis is within industry.

**Uncertainty is not 3.** If you are unsure, choose the nearest rating or CJ and write a note.

## Decision rules

Changes from v1.0 are marked **[new]** or **[changed]**.

### General

1. **Rate practice, not severity, and only what is stated.** Rate only what the narrative states or carries by an explicit attribution ("cause was…", "failed to…", "was not wearing…", "operated before the route was lined"). An event mechanism that *could* reflect a lapse but has no stated cause — wide gage, a shove over a switch, a broken rail, a head-on collision "under investigation" — is CJ, however severe. **A stated crew action or omission that caused the event is an attribution** and is rateable, usually 2 ("failed to drop pin", "handbrake was not set").  
2. **Frontline compliance counts.** Not wearing provided PPE, not following a procedure: 2. It becomes 3 only when the narrative also describes a discretionary response or a control that worked (rule 6). **[changed: follows from the new rule 6; confirm]**  
3. **Response actions count when they are discretionary.** Stop-work, placing the train in emergency on seeing a hazard, investigation findings, corrective action that changes how the work is done ("practices have been modified to prevent recurrence"), retraining, and conservative action beyond the requirement move the rating toward 4–5. Required acts do not (rules 8 to 8f).  
   - **3a. Discipline is not a favourable response.** **[new]** Suspension, dismissal or decertification of an individual confirms the lapse and changes nothing about procedure, supervision or conditions. Rate the stated lapse: 2.  
   - **3b. A crew's response to a hazard.** **[new]** The response alone is 4 (engineer saw fire under a car and stopped). The response together with a stated lapse by the organisation is 3 (improperly lined switch, crew placed the train in emergency).  
4. **Equipment or material failure alone is CJ** unless the narrative speaks to inspection, maintenance or a discretionary response. See 8b.  
5. **Attributions are evidence.** "Inadequate precautions", "lack of awareness", "failed to comply" are unfavourable. "Practices have been modified", "the area was dangered off in anticipation of a fall" are favourable.  
6. **Using 3, 1 and 2.** **[changed]** 3 requires both a stated lapse (rule 1) and a discretionary response or a control that worked (rule 3) in the same narrative. A lapse followed only by a required response is 2. A single frontline lapse is 2; use 1 when the narrative shows more (see the anchor for 1).  
7. **Disputes and boilerplate** ("submits this report solely to terminate the citation"): CJ unless practice is described. Whether a narrative that explicitly minimises an injury is 2 is **open, Q5**.

**ADDITION**: It isn't precisely a dispute, but there are somewhat common reports where the reporter is clearly dismissive of reporting requirements. So, 'I'm submitting this report so MHSA doesn't shut down my mine" would be evidence of a negative perception of reporting culture. 

### Required acts are baseline

8. **Mandatory reporting is baseline.** Notifying the inspector, filing an Event Notification or an FRA/MSHA report is the required act. It does not raise the rating, and on its own it is CJ. A later retraction of a notification is not evidence either way.  
   - **8a. Required operational responses are baseline.** Declaring a system inoperable, entering an LCO/TS action, posting a required fire watch or compensatory measure, declaring an emergency classification and evacuating under the emergency plan, tripping and stabilising the plant: CJ on their own. Rateable only with added discretionary or evaluative content.  
   - **8b. Equipment or instrument failure is CJ unless practice is stated.** Rateable when a human or organisational cause is stated (tagging or configuration-control error, test performance, work planning, a jumper installed contrary to procedure), usually 2, or when maintenance or inspection practice is described. Recurrence counts only when the narrative connects it ("despite the earlier corrective action", "the repair did not resolve").  
   - **8c. Report genres with no practice content are CJ.** Non-work-related events on site, Part 21 supplier-defect notifications, routine spill or environmental notifications, planned-outage and scheduled-maintenance notifications. Exception: the narrative describes what the organisation changed (a Part 21 report where the licensee "revised plant procedures to add the C-clips to the inspection list" is 4).  
   - **8d. Post-accident testing is baseline (rail).** **[adopted]** FRA-mandated toxicological testing ("crew was tested", "all tests were negative") is not evidence either way.  
   - **8e. Voluntary and courtesy notifications (nuclear).** **[new]** Rate 4 when the narrative itself calls the notification voluntary or a courtesy and that notification is what the report is about. A voluntary step mentioned in passing inside a required report does not count. Never 5 for reporting alone; 5 needs conservative action with investigation or learning.  
   - **8f. Required responses in mining are baseline.** **[new]** Posting, barricading, cribbing or "dangering off" a fall area after the fall, and notifying MSHA, are CJ on their own. **Report timing is excluded for now**: "reported the same day", "immediately reported", or a status change recorded later is not evidence either way. Exception: a hazard recognised and controlled *before* the event ("dangered off in anticipation of a fall") is 4.

&nbsp;  *(The number 8e was used in the 2026-11-05 draft for a proposal that fitness-for-duty reports be CJ. That proposal was rejected; see rule 12.)*

### Whose practice

9. **Third-party conduct is not the organisation's practice.** **[adopted, extended]** Motorists at crossings, trespassers, shippers, customers and other railroads: their conduct is CJ for the reporting organisation. Crossing devices "activated and functioning" and listed crossing protection are baseline (CJ); devices "not operating" is unfavourable. The organisation's own response is rateable under rule 3 (crew applied the emergency brake, sounded the horn, called 911 and gave first aid: 4).  
   - **9a. Contractors working under the railroad's authority are part of the organisation**, because the railroad controls the work.

- 9a MODIFICATION: Need clear statement that contractor is under railroad's authority, otherwise CJ. 

  
   - **9b. Aviation.** For a controller's report the organisation is the air traffic operation the reporter works in, including adjacent facilities, procedures and automation. Pilots, airlines, the military and other airspace users are third parties. Their conduct is **CJ unless the narrative has clear implications or recommendations for practices under air traffic control's own control**, including arrival and departure corridor rules. How to rate once it is rateable is **open, Q3**.

**CLARIFICATION: for instances of pilot / ATC miscommunication; if the pilot is the one miscommunicating (e.g., not switching to correct frequency; missing read back) it is CJ; if it is ATC, it is rate-able.** 

### Industry-specific

10. **Aviation (controller reports).** **[new]**  
    - **10a. Tone and self-criticism are evidence of climate.** The emotional tone of the report and the degree of self-criticism count. (The draft rule that recommendations and self-criticism are baseline was rejected.) How much they move the rating is **open, Q4**.  
    - **10b. Rate the conditions the narrative describes**: equipment, procedures, staffing, supervision, briefing and coordination.  
    - **10c. Conduct during the event is practice**, by the reporter or colleagues, rated under rules 2 and 3. A colleague speaking up, a go-around issued in time, or denying service to regain control count.  
    - **10d. Potential against current.** A description of conditions that *could* compromise safety but have not yet done so is rated highly (rule 13). Conditions that are currently affecting safety are rated as conditions under 10b. Where the line falls is **open, Q3**.

**REFINEMENT: All of 10 is generalizable, and b and c may be redundant to other rules. Elaborate that tone is often frustration that someone expresses in having their concerns not being acknowledged and acted on.** 

  
11. **Mining factor checklists.** **[new]** Some operators end every narrative with a fixed list ("PPE was not a factor, … miner proficiency was a factor").  
    - A line saying something was **not** a factor is form language. It is not evidence of good practice. A generic compliance disclaimer ("did not violate any laws") is treated the same way. Rate CJ.   
    - A cause attributed to the worker ("lack of awareness", "not paying attention", "improper lifting", "proficiency was a factor") is an attribution: 2.  
    - **A hedged factor counts the same as a certain one** ("may have been a factor", "maybe a factor"): 2.  
    - A specific control described in use still counts. Rate 4.   
12. **Fitness-for-duty reports (nuclear) are rateable.** **[new]** A failed, refused or admitted test is more than one individual's behaviour: the person thought it acceptable or expected not to be caught. The failure alone is unfavourable. Where a positive organisational response is described (a review of the person's past work), the narrative is favourable. Responses of access restricted or other punitive measures are required, and not considered positive indicators of culture. Which numbers these map to is **open, Q1**.

**UPDATE: the same rule should be applied to rail (or any other industry) but rail and nuclear are where this happens. May updated.** 

  
13. **Proactive identification of a potential risk is favourable (nuclear).** **[new]** A report of a condition that could compromise safety but has not caused an event — an unanalysed condition, a latent deficiency found by the licensee's own review — is 4. With in-depth analysis, planning or corrective action, 5. An actual failure or event described in a mandatory report is CJ under rules 8 to 8c, unless a cause or practice is stated. Where the line falls for equipment found degraded before it was needed is **open, Q2**.

## Edits that follow from the decisions and were not discussed

Please confirm these on Monday.

- **Report timing.** Rule 8f excludes report timing in mining. For consistency "prompt reporting" was removed from rule 3 and from the anchor for 4, and "immediately reported" from rule 5. This applies in every industry unless you want it limited to mining.

**CONFIRMED: Not report timing information considered.**

  
- **Rule 2 and the anchor for 3.** "Controls present but not used" was removed from the anchor for 3, because the new rule 6 requires a response or a control that worked. PPE provided but not worn is therefore 2.  
- **Discipline against retraining.** Rule 3a covers suspension, dismissal and decertification. Retraining stays a response under rule 3. Counselling ("engineer has been counseled") is not placed in either.  
- **Filing an aviation report.** Every aviation narrative is a voluntary report, so filing alone cannot tell narratives apart. Rule 10a counts tone and self-criticism, not the act of filing.  
- **Hedged causes outside checklists.** Rule 11 makes hedged checklist factors rateable. Whether "most likely human error" in a free-text narrative is treated the same way is in Q5.  
- **Rule 8f and the regulation.** Rule 8f was accepted on the understanding that posting a fall area and notifying MSHA are mandatory. This has not been checked against the regulation text.
